How these numbers enter the proof

The support of the squeezed square in direction (cos θ, sin θ) is s|cos θ| + (1/s)|sin θ|. Its mean width is twice the average of that support, or 4(s + 1/s)/π.

The quarter-turn exchanges the squeeze with its inverse. Averaging the matrices gives C = ((s + 1/s)/2) I. A standard Gaussian plus independent noise of covariance C² − I has the same law as the Gaussian multiplied by C. Convexity gives the width comparison. The general proof is in the paper.
